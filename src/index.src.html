We're five students from the American University of Bahrain, building a satellite the size of a drink can for Bahrain's first CanSat competition.

A real satellite

A CanSat does a satellite's job, squeezed into the size of a drink can.

Layer by layer

Cameras, a flight computer, a battery, sensors and a radio, stacked on 3D-printed decks.

Ready to fly

The shell, the lid, the antenna and a folded parachute. Designed and printed by our team.

Bahrain Bay

12 December. Ten university teams, one tower: the Four Seasons, 201 m tall.

Released

About 170 m up, from the sky bridge near the top.

Parachute open

One second of free fall, then it slows to about 7 metres a second.

Eyes down

One camera sees colour, the other sees heat: the water is cooler, roads and sand are warmer.

Talking to the ground

Every second it radios its height and readings to our ground station at the Hilton Garden Inn.

Landed

About 24 seconds from the top to the ground.

Land from the sea

Bahrain keeps growing into the sea. The tower we drop from stands on an island built on the water, and our CanSat looks at that coast up close.
